What it does

Two jobs, done on your own machine.

Oshabe is a free desktop app for meeting notes and voice dictation. It listens locally, so your audio never has to leave your computer.

Meeting notes

Your whole call, turned into notes you can send.

Oshabe records what plays on your computer next to your microphone, so it works with Zoom, Meet, Teams and browser calls. Nobody sees a bot join, and the recording itself is never saved.

How it works

From call to notes in three moves.

1

Start

Press your shortcut, use the tray menu, or click Start. Two level bars show that it hears you.

2

Talk

The transcript fills in as people speak, with You and Others labelled. Pause and resume whenever you need.

3

Stop

Oshabe writes the summary, decisions and action items. Copy them or export Word or PDF.

FAQ

Quick answers.

Is Oshabe really free?

Yes. There is no account, no subscription and no usage limit. Oshabe is open source under the MIT licence.

Does a bot join my Zoom, Meet or Teams call?

No. Oshabe records the audio playing on your own computer next to your microphone, so no one sees an extra attendee. Recording laws differ by country, so tell people when you take notes.

Does my audio get uploaded?

No. Speech recognition runs on your computer. For meetings Oshabe saves only the transcript text and never keeps the recording.

Do I need an API key, an account or Ollama?

No. A built-in offline model handles cleanup and summaries. You can switch to Ollama or your own provider in Settings.

Which systems does it support?

Windows 10 and 11 (64-bit and ARM), macOS on Apple Silicon and Intel, and Linux as AppImage, deb and rpm.

Can I export the notes to Word or PDF?

Yes. Each meeting exports to .docx or PDF. You can also set old notes to delete themselves after a number of days you choose.

Why does my English sometimes come out in another language?

Automatic language detection can guess wrong on accented speech. Set the language to English in Settings and Oshabe stays in English.

Who makes Oshabe?

Hikari Webworks in Hyderabad, India. It is a fork of the open-source Handy project, released under the MIT licence.
